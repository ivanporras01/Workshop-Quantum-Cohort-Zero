Your first quantum circuit

  1. Prepare: begin with one qubit in |0⟩, the north pole.
  2. Choose H: select Hadamard in the gate library, then click Add gate.
  3. Read the sphere: the arrow now points to |+⟩. Z measurement probabilities are 50% and 50%.
  4. Change basis to X: the + outcome becomes certain. Changing the display basis does not measure the state.
  5. Try Bell pair: load the example and step through H and CX. The local arrows move to the centers as the qubits become entangled.

What does a sphere show?

One qubit’s reduced state. A pure state lies on the surface. A mixed local state lies inside. For a Bell pair, each local vector is zero while the full two-qubit state is pure and entangled. Separate spheres do not show all correlations; read the joint probabilities too.

Why do gate order and phase matter?

The circuit runs left to right. H → Z → H takes |0⟩ to |1⟩. Rearranging gates can change the answer. Global phase is present in the statevector but invisible on a Bloch sphere; relative phases can change later outcomes.

Measurements and repeated shots

“Measure once” collapses the current preview. “Run to end” prepares the circuit again. Repeated shots sample fresh copies of the state at the current playback step, without changing the preview. X/Y labels use 0 for + and 1 for −.

Scope of this lab

Up to 128 unitary gates, three qubits and 10,000 shots. U(θ,φ,λ) plus controlled operations provides a universal gate toolbox. This educational model has no hardware noise, optical loss, mid-circuit classical control or real-device connection. Browser randomness is for learning.
