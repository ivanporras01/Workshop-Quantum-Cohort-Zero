Qubit technologies at a glance

The same |0⟩ and |1⟩ in the Bloch sphere can represent two energy levels, two spin states, or two paths for a photon. The table shows where each qubit lives and how engineers control it.

Inside five quantum technologies

Choose a technology. Start with the apparatus, then zoom in to see what represents |0⟩ and |1⟩ and how the initial state is prepared.

Superconducting transmon

Where the qubit lives

Top view of a transmon chip. Two metal pads form a capacitor and a Josephson junction bridges them. A nearby microwave line drives the circuit.

The qubit uses two energy levels of the whole circuit.

|0⟩
Lowest circuit energy level
|1⟩
Next circuit energy level
How it is produced and prepared
  1. Pattern superconducting metal pads and wiring on a wafer.
  2. Form a Josephson junction with two superconducting layers separated by a thin insulating barrier.
  3. Package the chip and cool it in a dilution refrigerator. Use its two lowest circuit energy levels and initialize the qubit near |0⟩.

Silicon spin

Where the qubit lives

Cross section showing patterned electrodes above a silicon device. The electric potential confines a single electron below the center gate.

Gate voltages confine one electron. Its spin stores the qubit.

|0⟩
One electron spin state
|1⟩
The other selected spin state
How it is produced and prepared
  1. Fabricate tiny gate electrodes above a silicon device.
  2. Cool the device and tune the gate voltages to confine one electron in a quantum dot.
  3. Use a magnetic field to separate two spin states in energy and prepare a known spin state.

Trapped ion

Where the qubit lives

Side view of an ion floating above electrodes in a vacuum chamber. A laser beam crosses the ion.

Electrode fields hold the charged atom in vacuum.

|0⟩
One internal ion state
|1⟩
A second internal ion state
How it is produced and prepared
  1. Remove an electron from an atom to make a positive ion.
  2. Hold the ion in vacuum using fields from trap electrodes, then cool it with laser light.
  3. Use light to prepare one of two chosen internal states of the ion.

Neutral atom

Where the qubit lives

Focused laser beams hold three individual neutral atoms. A separate beam addresses the central atom.

Each optical tweezer holds one neutral atom.

|0⟩
One internal atomic state
|1⟩
A second internal atomic state
How it is produced and prepared
  1. Cool a cloud of neutral atoms in vacuum.
  2. Load individual atoms into focused laser spots called optical tweezers.
  3. Use light to prepare one of two chosen internal states of each atom.

Photonic

Where the qubit lives

A source emits a photon into an optical circuit with two waveguide paths labeled zero and one.

Two optical paths encode the photon qubit.

|0⟩
Photon in path A
|1⟩
Photon in path B
How it is produced and prepared
  1. Generate a single photon, for example by exciting a semiconductor quantum dot that emits light.
  2. Couple the photon into an optical path, such as a waveguide on a photonic chip.
  3. Use two paths to encode |0⟩ and |1⟩. Launching the photon into one path prepares that basis state.

Why light matters

See and identify matter

Visible light lets us see. A spectrometer separates light by wavelength so we can identify atoms from the lines they emit or absorb. These four lines are examples from hydrogen.

410, 434, 486, and 656 nm

Carry information

Infrared light carries signals through optical fiber. In a quantum link, one photon can carry a qubit in two polarization states. Polarization describes the orientation of the light’s electric field.

Control and read qubits

Laser pulses can change the states of trapped atoms. A detector can read some atomic qubits by seeing whether they fluoresce. Superconducting qubits use another part of the same spectrum: microwaves.

What we can demonstrate at the bench

Amplitude · change the height

With one BNC cable, change Vpp and hold frequency fixed. The trace gets taller. For a calibrated resonant qubit drive, pulse strength and duration help set the rotation angle. A quantum state amplitude is a complex coefficient, and its squared magnitude gives an outcome probability in the chosen basis.

Frequency · count cycles

Set 1 kHz, then 2 kHz. One cycle shrinks from 1 ms to 0.5 ms. A drive near a qubit’s transition frequency couples its two energy levels, whose gap is ΔE = hf. The bench compares timing at kilohertz. Superconducting qubit drives typically use gigahertz microwaves.

Phase · compare two traces

Use two synchronized channels at the same frequency. At 2 kHz, a quarter cycle is 125 µs (90°). Equal sine waves add at 0° and cancel at 180° in the scope’s sum. In a resonant qubit control model, drive phase selects the rotation axis in the XY plane. Relative state phase can change interference outcomes, as the H → Z → H guide shows.
